M.Tech project · IIT Kharagpur · Industrial & Systems Engineering

When the berth is not ready,
what should the ship do?

A schedule recovery model for container liners, built on real Maersk schedules for the TP8 and TP7 transpacific services. Each late vessel gets an exact optimisation of speed, port omission and berth handling. We check it against the decisions Maersk actually made, then train a model that recommends the same recovery in milliseconds.

04

Machine learning, used where it is honest

Real data has 52 sea legs and only three omitted calls, so a synthetic year for the whole fleet is generated from the real patterns. The delay predictor is still always tested on real ships, and the skip advisor learns from optimiser answers.

ML-1 · Next-port delay predictor

Tested on real ships

Six regression models on departure delay, planned leg time, schedule buffer and ports. Each is trained twice, on real legs only and on real + synthetic legs, and always tested on a real ship it has never seen.

ML-2 · Recovery advisor

MILP-labelled
  1. Sample thousands of situations from the real + synthetic delay and congestion patterns
  2. Solve each one exactly with the MILP
  3. Learn which calls the MILP omits: ten models, from logistic regression to XGBoost
  4. Advise: fix the skips, let a small MILP set the speeds

Where the training data comes from

Synthetic

The real files hold 6 ships over 5 months. One full year is simulated for the whole fleet, using the real delay, berth-congestion, speed-recovery and port-stay patterns. The real data itself is left untouched.

Next steps for the final thesis

  • Collect schedules for the remaining fleet: about 3 more TP8 and about 5 more TP7 vessels, over 12 months.
  • Verify vessel TEU and design speed (Equasis) and replace the assumed fuel curve with the vessels' own curves.
  • Add AIS speed tracks to separate anchorage waiting from slow steaming.
  • Calibrate rollover and delay costs with the supervisor, or recover them from Maersk's revealed choices.
  • Extend from one vessel to the whole service, since berth slots are shared between vessels.
